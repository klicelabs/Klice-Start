## Mission
Give people a calm, beautiful, and controlled beginning to every browser session.

Perch turns the browser's new tab from an empty or noisy surface into a personal home for orientation, organization, and daily momentum. The new tab is seen many times every day; that frequency is the product's greatest asset and its strictest quality constraint — small defects compound, and so does small craft.

## Vision
Perch becomes the browser home for people who care about their digital environment. It is the product they install on every new machine because the browser feels incomplete without it.

The vision is not "a better speed dial." It is the redefinition of a neglected surface into a category people treat as personal infrastructure.

## Values
Values are not aspirations. They are the filters that resolve conflicts when speed, growth, and quality pull in different directions.

| Value | What it means in practice | What it rules out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taste is a product capability | Craft is treated as a competitive function, not decoration. It is staffed, scheduled, and reviewed. | "Ship it ugly, fix it later." |
| User control is non-negotiable | The user's space, data, and defaults remain theirs. Automation asks, never acts silently. | Mandatory accounts for local value, dark patterns. |
| Privacy is part of quality | A feature that compromises privacy is not a quality feature, regardless of polish. | Data resale, attention harvesting, opaque cloud defaults. |
| Simplicity is protected by refusal | What we do not build is as decisive as what we build. | Feature creep disguised as completeness. |
| Craft is a business strategy | Perceived quality is the primary growth and retention lever, not an output of scale. | Treating polish as a post-launch phase. |
| Performance is respect | The new tab must feel instant. Latency is a product defect, not an engineering detail. | Loading states that delay orientation. |
| Long-term trust beats short-term extraction | We do not monetize attention, confusion, or dependency. | Ads, sponsored tiles, engagement-bait. |

## Company Principles
Principles translate values into daily decisions.

1. Build the product people would miss immediately.
2. Win through quality before scale.
3. Make the default experience excellent before adding configuration.
4. Treat every visible detail as part of the brand.
5. Prefer durable decisions over convenient decisions.
6. Never monetize user attention, user data, or user confusion.

## Long-Term Vision
Perch evolves from a beautiful new tab into the personal browser dashboard category leader: the place where people begin, organize, search, resume, and understand their digital work.

The product may become more intelligent, more connected, and more extensible, but it must never stop feeling personal, fast, calm, and owned by the user. Expansion is justified only when it strengthens orientation, ownership, beauty, and habit.

## Long-Term Thinking Framework
Every material decision must be evaluated across three time horizons. When the horizons conflict, the longer horizon wins unless the short-term cost is existential.

| Horizon | Question | Failure looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short term (now–6 months) | Does this keep the product healthy, fast, and trustworthy today? | Shipping a regression to hit a date. |
| Medium term (6 months–2 years) | Does this strengthen the category position, brand, and paid value? | Optimizing a metric that erodes perceived quality. |
| Long term (2–10 years) | Does this compound trust, taste, and defensibility? | Sacrificing the privacy stance for a growth shortcut. |

**The rule:** Never sacrifice the vision for convenience. A decision that wins in the short frame but loses in the long frame is a decision against the company. The reverse is not always true — a long-term bet that risks survival in the short frame must be staged, not abandoned, but it still wins the framing.

## Success Definition
Perch wins when it becomes a reference product: loved for its quality, trusted for its restraint, and recognized as the standard for browser homes. Revenue is evidence of success, not its definition.
